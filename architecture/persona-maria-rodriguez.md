# Persona: Maria Rodriguez - The Overwhelmed Caregiver

## 💡 Why Personas Matter for Product Design

Personas represent your real users and help the team make design decisions that solve actual problems. This persona prevents you from building features YOU would want instead of what YOUR USER needs.

### 🎯 How to Use This Information:

1. **Before every feature decision:** Ask "Would Maria understand this?" and "Does this solve Maria's problem?"
2. **For UI/UX choices:** Check her tech literacy (smartphone-only), language needs (Spanish/English), and reading level (6th grade)
3. **For feature prioritization:** Focus on her pain points first - medication management, warning signs, transportation help
4. **For testing:** Validate that your design works for someone with limited literacy, high stress, and no desktop computer

**⚡ Quick Rule:** If Maria can't use it while juggling two kids and worrying about her mom, redesign it.

## The Scenario

Maria's mother was recently discharged from the hospital after heart surgery. The discharge paperwork is 15 pages of medical jargon that Maria struggles to understand. She received verbal instructions from multiple nurses and doctors, but studies show she'll likely forget 40-80% of what was said. Maria is terrified of making a mistake with her mother's medications or missing warning signs of complications. She needs to get her mother to follow-up appointments but has no car and doesn't know about available transportation services. She's overwhelmed, scared, and needs help immediately.

---

## Key Pain Points & Challenges

### Medical Information Overload
Discharge documents are too complex, filled with medical terminology she doesn't understand. Feels overwhelmed by the volume of information.

### Memory & Retention
Research shows caregivers forget 40-80% of verbal instructions given at discharge. Maria has no reliable way to remember critical information.

### Medication Management Fear
Terrified of making dosing errors. Complex medication schedules with multiple drugs, different timing, and specific instructions.

### Transportation Barriers
No car and limited knowledge of public transportation or medical transport services. Follow-up appointments seem impossible to reach.

### Language & Literacy Barriers
Spanish is primary language, 6th grade reading level in English. Most medical documents are in complex English.

### Lack of Warning Sign Awareness
Doesn't know what symptoms indicate an emergency vs. normal recovery. Constantly worried but unsure when to call for help.

## Goals & Success Criteria

### Information Needs
- Plain-language discharge instructions
- Visual aids and color-coding system
- Bilingual support (Spanish/English)
- Clear warning signs with pictures

### Functional Needs
- Medication reminders with timing
- Appointment scheduling assistance
- Transportation resource finder
- Emergency contact quick access

### Emotional Needs
- Reduced anxiety and overwhelm
- Confidence in caregiving abilities
- Connection to support community
- Reassurance she's doing it right

## Design Implications for Care to Home Companion

### Mobile-First Design
App must work perfectly on smartphones with limited data plans

### Large Touch Targets
Easy to tap buttons and controls for stressed, multi-tasking users

### Visual Communication
Icons, colors, and images instead of text-heavy interfaces

### Simple Language
6th grade reading level, avoid medical jargon

### Bilingual Support
Easy language switching between Spanish and English

### Clear Visual Hierarchy
Most important information always visible and prominent

### Push Notifications
Proactive reminders for medications and appointments

### Offline Capability
Core features work without internet connection
