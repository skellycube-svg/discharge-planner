DISCHARGE CONTEXT SUMMARIZATION — How and why we transform the CSV
==================================================================

Source CSV: discharge-planner/data/discharge_instructions_combined.csv
Code:       src/app/data/discharge.ts
            src/app/data/plainLanguage.ts
            src/app/data/types.ts

The persona we are designing for (Maria Rodriguez, per CLAUDE.md and
DESIGN.md): single mom of two, Spanish-primary, ~6th-grade reading
level, smartphone-only, post-discharge stress, forgets 40-80% of
verbal instructions, fears medication mistakes. Every decision below
is filtered through "would Maria understand this and does it solve a
Maria pain point."

The CSV was authored for clinicians. We need it to be readable by
Maria. So we don't show fields raw — we transform them.


----------------------------------------------------------------
COLUMN-BY-COLUMN: WHAT WE DO WITH EACH FIELD
----------------------------------------------------------------

patient_id
  Used as the routing key. Stored in localStorage by PatientContext so
  the demo survives a refresh. Not displayed.

patient_name
  Shown verbatim in the Dashboard hero ("Caring for {name}"), in the
  top bar avatar/initials, and in the Resources personalization banner
  ("Picked for {first name}"). First-person framing puts the child at
  the center of the screen instead of the diagnosis.

mrn
  Shown on the Login picker and in the top bar so a caregiver can
  confirm they picked the right patient. Not used elsewhere.

weight_kg
  Parsed and stored. Not surfaced yet — it would be the basis for
  weight-dosed med instructions (e.g., "As directed by weight"), which
  we deliberately did not invent numbers for. Available for future
  work.

preferred_language
  Drives the Resources personalization (mapped to a language code and
  matched against each program's Languages column). NOT used to flip
  the app's UI language by itself — Maria might be Spanish-preferred
  but reading EN with help from a relative, so we let the toggle be
  manual and obvious.

allergies
  Surfaced as the highest-priority banner on the Dashboard:
    - If allergies != "NKA": red ALERT banner with the allergen
      printed large.
    - If allergies == "NKA": small green "No known allergies"
      reassurance banner.
  Reason: a forgotten allergy is the kind of mistake Maria fears
  most. It earns a place above everything except patient identity.

admission_date / discharge_date
  Parsed and stored. Not surfaced in the demo. Could be added to a
  timeline view later; not load-bearing for the happy path.

admitting_diagnosis
  Stored. We default the headline to discharge_diagnosis since that
  represents what the child is going home with.

discharge_diagnosis
  This is the SINGLE biggest transformation in the app.
  Raw value (e.g., "Acute Gastroenteritis") would fail the 6th-grade
  reading rule. We pass it through plainLanguage.ts:
    - plainDiagnosis()       -> "Stomach bug" (and Spanish variant
                                 "Infección estomacal")
    - plainWhatHappened()    -> a one-sentence plain-language
                                 explanation of what occurred and what
                                 to do, in EN and ES.
  Both are shown on the Dashboard "What happened" panel. The raw
  clinical phrase does not appear anywhere on Maria's path.
  We also feed discharge_diagnosis into the Resources scorer to pick
  programs that fit the care plan.

condition_on_discharge
  Surfaced verbatim with an "Activity" icon ("How they are doing:
  Improved") because the words in the CSV are already short and
  layperson-friendly ("Stable", "Good", "Improved").

primary_care_physician
  Shown on the Dashboard "Call the doctor" card and on each yellow
  CAUTION row in Warning Signs. Phone number is a placeholder
  ((555) 123-4567) because the CSV has no phone column — the field is
  ready when real numbers exist.

consulting_physicians / discharge_authenticated_by
  Parsed and stored. Not surfaced. They have no immediate Maria value
  and would clutter the screen.

diet
  Stored. Not surfaced in the demo because every row in the CSV is
  "Regular, Age Appropriate" — there is nothing to communicate. As
  soon as a row breaks that pattern, this becomes a card.

seek_medical_attention_for
  This is the OTHER big transformation.
  The raw column is a comma-separated list of clinical phrases like
  "Persistent Vomiting > 24 hours, Signs of dehydration, Severe
  abdominal pain". We:
    1. Split it on commas (carefully — some phrases contain
       parenthesized commas, so we split on commas not inside parens).
    2. For each phrase, translate it to plain language via
       plainSymptom() (EN + ES) — e.g.,
       "Persistent Temp > 100.4 F" -> "Fever over 100.4°F that won't
       go down" / "Fiebre de más de 100.4°F que no baja".
    3. Classify it as 'emergency' or 'warning' via classifyWarning(),
       which keyword-matches against a list of life-threatening signs
       (breathing trouble, chest pain, seizures, lethargy, swelling of
       face, blood sugar > 300, blood in stool, dehydration,
       self-injurious behavior, etc.).
  This drives the Warning Signs page split:
    - Red STOP block -> emergency items, each with a tel:911 button.
    - Yellow CAUTION block -> non-emergency items, each with a
      tel:{pcpPhone} button.
  This is the most important screen in the app for Maria's pain
  point of "fearing she won't recognize a real emergency."

special_instructions
  Stored. Could be surfaced as a "this week" checklist; not in the
  hackathon demo to keep the screen count tight.

procedures
  Semicolon-split into a string[]. Stored, not surfaced. It belongs
  on a "what happened in the hospital" detail screen, which is out of
  scope.

educational_forms_given
  Semicolon-split into a string[]. Stored, not surfaced. Same reason.

home_medications  +  new_prescriptions
  Combined and parsed into a typed Medication[]. For each piped entry
  we extract:
    - rawName               (everything before the parenthesis)
    - plainName             (e.g., "Acetaminophen" -> "Pain & fever
                             medicine (Tylenol)") via simplifyMedName.
                             Mapping is a small dictionary so we never
                             hallucinate a brand name.
    - dose / route / frequency  (the three comma-separated chunks
                             inside the parenthesis)
    - iconType              ('food' | 'water' | 'topical' | 'inhaler'
                             | 'pill') inferred from keywords
                             ("cream"/"topical" -> topical, "inhaler"/
                             "puff"/"HFA" -> inhaler, "with food"/
                             "before breakfast" -> food, "suspension"/
                             "solution"/"drops"/"syrup" -> water,
                             else pill). Maria recognizes pictures.
    - timeOfDay             ('morning' | 'afternoon' | 'evening' |
                             'asNeeded') inferred from "bedtime",
                             "morning", "PRN", "twice daily", etc.
                             Used to group med cards.
    - amount                inferred from "(N puffs)", "(N pills)",
                             default 1. Drives drawn pill icons so
                             Maria can count.
    - notePlain             a friendly sentence stitched from
                             keywords ("Take with food.", "Two times
                             a day.", "Breathe in through the
                             inhaler.", "Use only for a long
                             seizure.", etc.). Always concrete and
                             imperative.
    - isPrescription        true for new_prescriptions entries — drives
                             the indigo "New prescription" badge so
                             Maria can tell what she still needs to
                             pick up.
  We deliberately do NOT compute dose volumes for weight-dosed meds,
  because guessing those is the exact mistake Maria fears.

appointments
  Pipe-split. Each chunk parsed into:
    - dateISO               ISO string parsed from "MM/DD/YYYY HH:MM
                             AM/PM" so date-fns can format it nicely
                             ("Tuesday · April 16").
    - specialty / doctor / location  via the regex
                             "^(.*?)\s+with\s+(.*?)\s+at\s+(.*)$".
  Each appointment renders as a big card with the day/date called out
  in the indigo header band, plus a green "I need a ride" button that
  routes to /resources — wiring Maria's transportation pain point
  directly into the social-needs flow.


----------------------------------------------------------------
WHY WE ADDED FIELDS THAT DON'T EXIST IN THE CSV
----------------------------------------------------------------

  hasAllergies       — boolean derived from allergies != "NKA". Saves
                       the dashboard from string-comparing in JSX.
  diagnosisPlain     — pre-baked plain-language headline so we don't
                       call the mapping function in three places.
  whatHappenedPlain  — same idea for the explanatory paragraph.
  pcpPhone           — placeholder ((555) 123-4567) until the CSV has
                       a phone column. Wiring is real (tel: links work);
                       only the digits are fake.
  Medication.iconType / timeOfDay / amount / color / notePlain
                     — UI affordances inferred from the raw name +
                       sig. They live on the data object so the
                       component can stay dumb (no string-sniffing
                       in JSX).


----------------------------------------------------------------
WHAT WE DROPPED OR DELIBERATELY DID NOT DO
----------------------------------------------------------------

  - We do not hallucinate translations for medical content we don't
    have a mapping for. If a diagnosis is missing from
    diagnosisPlainMap, we fall back to the raw label rather than
    invent a Spanish phrase.
  - We do not auto-translate via an LLM at runtime. Hackathon scope,
    and we'd rather render confident English with strong icons than
    wrong Spanish.
  - We do not show admission date, consulting physicians, procedures,
    educational forms, or special instructions on the demo path.
    They are clinically useful but not Maria-actionable, and adding
    them would push the most important content (allergies, meds,
    warnings) further down the screen.
  - We do not invent dose volumes for weight-dosed meds. Showing
    "5 mL" when the CSV says "As directed by weight" would be
    wrong and would actively cause the harm Maria fears.
  - We do not compute a single "next thing to do" timeline yet. The
    schedule object exists in the codebase but is intentionally not
    wired in this demo, since it would be guessed rather than
    grounded in the CSV.


----------------------------------------------------------------
ONE-LINE SUMMARY OF THE PHILOSOPHY
----------------------------------------------------------------

Read the CSV literally; render it for Maria.

Where the CSV is already plain (allergies, condition_on_discharge,
patient_name), pass it through unchanged. Where it is clinical
(discharge_diagnosis, seek_medical_attention_for, home_medications),
translate it to a 6th-grade plain-language form via small, hand-
authored mappings. Never invent numbers or instructions that aren't
already in the data. Surface the most safety-relevant fields
(allergies, warning signs, doctor's number) at the top of the
hierarchy on every screen.
